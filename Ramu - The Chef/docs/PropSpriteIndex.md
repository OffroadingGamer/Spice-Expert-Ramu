Kitchen Essentials pack. Generated by `tools/slice_props.py` +
`tools/prop-names.json`, so **the numbering is derived from the art, not typed
by hand**, and re-running the tool reproduces it exactly.

**Where the images are.** `Art/_sliced/02 - Kitchen Props/`, inside the
gitignored `Art/` tree, so none of it is committed:

- `kp1/001.png` … one transparent PNG per item, recoloured, tightly cropped
- `_recoloured/*.png` — the four whole sheets after the palette transfer
- `CONTACT_kp1_01.png` … eleven numbered **and named** contact pages to read
- `manifest.json` — every item's source box and size, plus the 57-entry palette map

**Reading the ids.** `KP1-05` is Kitchen Props sheet 1, item 05. The prefix is
`KP`, not `P`, deliberately: `P-08` style labels already exist in
[PropList.md](PropList.md) §1a as superseded eyeballed guesses, and reusing
`P` would quietly collide with them.

## 4. What the tool had to solve

### 4.1 The recolour — a statistical transfer, not a quantise

Kitchen Props is a strict **57-colour palette with binary alpha**. Kitchen
Essentials has **~450,000 distinct colours** across its three sheets: it is
painterly art, not indexed pixel art. So there is no discrete Essentials
palette to snap onto, and matching had to be statistical.

What actually separates the two packs, measured in CIELAB over every opaque
pixel:

| | Essentials | Props, before | Props, after |
|---|---|---|---|
| **b\*** (yellow ↔ blue) | **+17.4** | **+2.3** | **+12.3** |
| **chroma** | **22.1** | **12.1** | **21.3** |
| a\* | +5.5 | +9.2 | +9.5 |
| mean L | 45.8 | 53.6 | 53.4 |

Props was **15 b\* units too cool and 10 chroma units too flat**. The transfer
closes about three quarters of the warmth gap and nearly all of the chroma gap.

Two choices in `build_lut()` are worth keeping:

- **The neutral ramp is measured from the low-chroma half of Essentials pixels
  at each lightness, not from all of them.** Using every pixel drags a\*
  negative in the highlights, because the pack's sage-green items live up
  there — and that tints white ceramic a sickly green. Neutral has to mean
  neutral.
- **Lightness gets a gentle monotone stretch, never a histogram match.** A full
  match drags the props midtones far darker, because Essentials is small
  outlined objects (lots of dark pixels) while Props is large furniture faces.
  That is a difference of *content*, not of palette. Mean L moves 53.6 → 53.4,
  which is the point.

Each colour keeps its own hue *identity* — its offset from its own pack's
neutral norm — so plants stay green and jars stay blue; only the base they sit
on moves. **57 colours in, 57 distinct colours out, zero collisions**, and
every pixel of all four sheets mapped with none left over. The shading
structure and the crisp pixel edges are untouched.

### 4.2 The segmentation — periodicity, not valleys

The Essentials slicer works on plain connected components. That fails here:
Props is laid out as **runs of touching tiles**, and sixteen cabinets sharing
their edges label as a single 512px component with no empty column to cut on.

The obvious fix — cut at the column with the least ink — is wrong, and wrong
in a way that looks right until you read the output. It also cuts **chairs in
half at the waist** between seat and legs, and separates **plants from their
pots**. What tells a run of tiles from one tall object is not the depth of the
valley but the **periodicity** of the ink profile:

| | axis | period | tiles | autocorrelation | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| run of 16 cabinets | columns | 32 | 16 | **0.84** | split |
| 4 sink counters | columns | 32 | 4 | **0.75** | split |
| 3 stacked tables | rows | 32 | 3 | **0.64** | split |
| one chair | columns | — | — | 0.19 | left alone |
| one chair | rows | — | — | −0.08 | left alone |

Wide enough separation that the threshold sits at 0.55 with nothing near it.
**Every split that fired across all four sheets landed on period 32** — the
pack's grid pitch — which is the external check that the segmentation is right.

Two more details that mattered:

- **The cuts are placed on the grid, not at the local minimum.** Two isometric
  tiles interlock diagonally, so their bounding boxes overlap and the
  least-ink column sits a few pixels off the grid line. Snapping to it left
  every crop carrying a **4px sliver of its neighbour**. Cutting on the exact
  32px pitch produced boxes at 224, 256, 288, 320… all exactly 32 wide.
- **The annotation filter needed a lightness test.** Two of the four sheets are
  assembly diagrams carrying the words PACK / SINGLE / FOR THIS and arrows.
  Glyphs are flat single-colour shapes, so "small and flat" finds them — but
  it also found the **glass window panes on KP1**, which are one flat colour
  too. The panes measure **L 86** against the glyphs' **L 6**, so requiring the
  flat colour to be dark separates them with nothing in between. Without that
  test, five real sprites were being silently deleted.

## 5. ⚠️ The finding that matters more than any of the above

**The two packs are not the same art style, and no palette work will make them
one.** Recolouring fixed the colour; it cannot fix this:

| | Kitchen Essentials | Kitchen Props |
|---|---|---|
| median item size | **94 × 124 px** | **32 × 32 px** |
| rendering | painterly, soft shading, thick dark outlines, ~450k colours | flat pixel art, 57 colours, hard 1px outlines |
| projection | front elevation | **isometric** |
| alpha | antialiased | binary |

That is roughly a **4× resolution difference plus a different drawing
technique plus a different projection.** Put a recoloured Props chair beside an
Essentials handi and it reads as two games.

**This is a decision, not a defect**, and it is the user's to make. The honest
options:

1. **Props for the room, Essentials for the line.** The isometric furniture
   becomes background set-dressing at its own scale — a kitchen behind the
   belt — while every ingredient and station on the conveyor stays Essentials.
   Two registers, deliberately separated by depth. Cheapest, and the recolour
   is exactly what makes it hold together.
2. **Props only, and rebuild the line isometric.** Coherent, and the pack has a
   stove (KP1-76), a sink (KP1-77) and a mixer (KP1-94) — but it has **no
   ingredients at all**, and the whole interaction graph in
   [PropList.md](PropList.md) §4 is built on Essentials ingredient sprites.
   This is a rewrite, with 15 days left and a live entry scoring.
3. **Essentials only.** Props stays unused. Nothing is lost that the game
   currently needs — which is the uncomfortable part worth saying plainly.

**✅ DECIDED by the user, Sep 4 2026, 22:05 IST — option 1.**

> *"Props are for the room and Essentials are for the line. Isometric furniture as
> background set-dressing behind the belt, at its own scale, with depth doing the
> separating."*

It is the only option that spends the \$4 without spending the fortnight, and the
depth separation makes the scale difference read as intentional rather than as a
mistake. **What follows from it:**

- The belt, the ingredients and the four stations stay **Essentials**, so the
  eleven-transform graph in [PropList.md](PropList.md) §4 is untouched.
- **Props never sits beside a line sprite**, which is what makes the 4× scale gap
  a depth cue instead of an error. Keep them in separate render layers and never
  scale a Props tile up to match — upscaled 32px pixel art next to painterly art
  reads worse than the honest size difference.
- **KayKit Phase 2a is cut** (Plan items 12 and 51). A third art source, rendered
  from 3D at a fourth resolution and a fourth technique, is exactly what this
  section argues against.
- The most useful items for a background are the counter runs (**KP1-78 … KP1-83**),
  the cabinets and fridges (**KP1-84 … KP1-91**), the chairs and tables
  (**KP1-96 … KP1-114**), and the KP3 corner pieces for turning a counter run.

## 6. What the two diagram sheets are

KP3 and KP4 are the pack's **instructions**, not its assets. Their tiles are
KP1 pieces shown in runs, with arrows and captions pointing at the result. The
slicer strips the captions and numbers what is left, so they are catalogued
here for completeness, but there are only two things in them KP1 does not have:

- **KP3-19 / KP3-20** — L-shaped corner counters
- **KP3-29 / KP3-30** — U-shaped corner counters

Both carry a small arrow glued on, because the arrow touches the tile and
merges with it as one component. Crop before use.
