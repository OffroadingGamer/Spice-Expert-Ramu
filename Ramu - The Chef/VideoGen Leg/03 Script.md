# 03 Script — "The First Glass" (working title; alt: "Back on the Line")

Written 19:55 IST. Portrait 9:16. Target 80–90 s, 12 shots. One character across five kitchens. No dialogue; narration only where marked (Ramu's "warm" voice, past tense, few words). The ending was written first.

## The story in one breath

A boy pours tea at a roadside stall in a small town. He goes back to work every dawn — at a highway dhaba, at a city Italian kitchen, through the night the kitchen closes and he sits on the step with his knife roll — until he's head chef of a grand hotel in Delhi. The first thing he does there, at dawn, is pour tea for his brigade. Same glass. Back to work.

Theme lock: "back to work" is the refrain — every stage opens at dawn with him going back in. The rise is the arc; the return is the theme.

## Cinematic grammar (three registers, used deliberately)

| Register | Where | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Naturalist** (Pursuit-of-Happyness-style) | the work: stalls, dhaba, Italian line | handheld, shallow depth of field, tight close-ups on the face and hands, warm practical light (bulbs, tandoor glow, sodium street light), over-the-shoulder walking shots, no music stings — sound of work |
| **Shadow realism** (Dhurandhar-style) | the low point and the pressure beats | one hard light source, silhouette, deep blacks, smoke and steam as texture, very slow push-in, sweat visible, stark contrast |
| **Symmetry tableau** (Wes-Anderson-style) | the chapter transitions and the ending | dead-centre framing, flat-on composition, overhead "God's-eye" inserts of objects laid out (tea glasses, knife roll, brigade plates), held still, controlled palette |

Why three: the naturalist register makes the emotion human; the shadow register makes the fall hurt; the symmetry register makes the rise feel *earned and orderly* — chaos to control is the visual arc. Portrait favours all three: close-ups fill a vertical frame, overheads read cleanly, a single centred figure is the natural 9:16 composition.

Rule for every prompt: **never name the films or directors in generated text** — describe the technique. Style references by name are fine in our own notes only.

## The ending (written first)

**Shot 12 — Dawn, grand hotel kitchen, Delhi.** Overhead symmetrical tableau: a steel counter, twelve small tea glasses in a perfect row, one steel kettle. Ramu's hands — older, scarred — pour the first glass. Cut to a centred, flat-on wide: Ramu in head-chef whites, the same faded bandana, standing alone in an immaculate silent kitchen, brigade filing in behind him out of focus. He picks up the glass, drinks, ties the bandana tighter. Card: **"Back to work."** Hold. Black.

Narration (final line only): *"Nine kitchens. Same glass."*
