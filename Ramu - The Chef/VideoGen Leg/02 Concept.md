## A — The Last Ticket (Ramu) — RECOMMENDED

**Logline.** On his first night back after the restaurant nearly closed, a line cook fights an order rail alone, nearly loses one ticket, and saves it — then pins up tomorrow's first ticket and goes home to do it again.

**Arc.** Open on the rail: tickets clipped, crawling toward the pass — the job is the first image. Ramu ties the bandana, lights the tandoor. Rush builds: grill, tandoor, fryer, hands never still. One ticket slips down the rail unfinished; he catches it at the pass, plates with shaking hands. Beat of silence. The rail is empty. He wipes the pass, pins tomorrow's first ticket, lights go down. Turn: the near-loss. Ending: the pinned ticket — going back to work is the last shot, not the first.

**Why it wins.** Literal theme (back to work, a job) in the first frame; a complete arc with a quiet, earned ending; one strong visual motif (the ticket rail) that carries the whole piece. Reuses a character with existing, RUN-generated art for style reference. Doubles as promotion for the game (a line in the description, if the user wants).

**Size.** 1 episode, ~8–10 shots, 60–75 s. Cast 1, locations 2 (the line, the pass). Already drafted in the Studio as "The Last Ticket" — step 2 complete, no credits spent.

## B — Night Shift, Signal Box

**Logline.** A railway signaller returning after a long medical leave has to route a delayed express through a junction alone on her first night back — and does it, one lever at a time.

**Arc.** Empty signal box, one lamp, a hand hesitating over the levers. The board lights up: the express is early and a freight is late. She talks herself through it out loud, the old rhythm coming back. The express clears. She writes the time in the log, exactly as she always did. Turn: the conflict on the board. Ending: the pen in the log — the job is hers again.

**Why it could win.** Strong, unusual setting; physical, tactile work; a single night, a single character, quiet dignity. Risk: railway signalling is hard for a generator to draw plausibly, and the levers/board need consistency across shots.

**Size.** 1 episode, ~8–10 shots, 60–75 s. Cast 1, locations 1–2. Would require a new project (recreate at step 1).

## C — First Fare

**Logline.** A laid-off office worker's first night driving a cab: a rude fare, a lost fare, then a passenger who leaves a note that makes the night worth it.

**Arc.** Ex-office lanyard swinging from the mirror. Three fares, three moods. The last passenger says nothing, tips nothing, leaves a scrap of paper: "You drive well. Better than my son." He laughs, pulls the lanyard down, drives on. Turn: the note. Ending: the empty mirror hook.

**Why it could win.** Warm, human, universally readable; the theme is explicit ("back to work" after a job loss). Risk: the most generic of the three — likely several cab/gig-work entries in the field; needs 4 faces (driver + 3 passengers), the most consistency work.

**Size.** 1 episode, ~9–11 shots, 75–90 s. Cast 4, locations 2. New project.

## Recommendation

**A.** It is the only one with a head start (drafted, zero spend), the only one with cleared reference art, and the strongest single visual motif. B is the artistic dark horse if the user wants something no one else will submit; C is the safest theme read and the most work.
